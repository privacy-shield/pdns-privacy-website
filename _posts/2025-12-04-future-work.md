      <h2 class="section-heading">Ideas &amp; Roadmap</h2>
      <p>
        Passive DNS Privacy Shield is an evolving project. Beyond its current capabilities, we see several
        promising directions that can further strengthen privacy, usability, and deployment flexibility.
      </p>
      <div class="content">
        <h3>Privacy-First Data Transport</h3>
        <p>
          To further minimize metadata exposure during data exchange, we plan to explore <strong>Tor-based pDNS data transfers</strong>. Routing pDNS submissions through anonymity networks can reduce linkability between sensors and collectors, adding an additional layer of privacy for operators and contributors in sensitive environments.
        </p>
        <h3>Mobile Device pDNS Sensors</h3>
        <p>
          Modern DNS traffic increasingly originates from mobile devices. A future goal is to develop a <strong>mobile pDNS sensor</strong> capable of collecting passive DNS data directly from smartphones regardless of which network they are connected to.
          This would enable privacy-aware DNS visibility for mobile ecosystems while preserving user transparency and consent.
        </p>
        <h3>Broader Embedded Device Support</h3>
        <p>
          Many DNS-active devices today operate outside traditional servers and workstations. We aim to expand sensor support to <strong>embedded and consumer devices</strong>, such as: 
        <ul>
          <li>Network-attached storage (NAS)</li>
          <li>CCTV and IP cameras</li>
          <li>Media players and smart TVs</li>
          <li>Other IoT and edge devices</li>
        </ul>
        </p>
        <p>
          Extending coverage to these platforms will improve the representativeness of collected pDNS data while
          keeping privacy safeguards intact.
        </p>
        <h3>Simplified Deployment &amp; Packaging</h3>
        <p>
          To lower the barrier to adoption, we plan to introduce a <strong>packaging system and one-click installer</strong>.
          This will allow operators to deploy the full Passive DNS Privacy Shield stack - from sensors to collectors - quickly
          and consistently, even in non-expert environments.
        </p>
        <h3>Operational Awareness &amp; Notifications</h3>
        <p>
          Operating pDNS infrastructure at scale requires visibility. Future versions may include
          <strong>notification and alerting capabilities</strong>, such as:
        </p>
        <ul>
          <li>Detection of unusually large data volumes</li>
          <li>Component health status changes</li>
          <li>Service disruptions or degraded performance</li>
        </ul>
        <p>
          These features will help operators maintain reliable, transparent, and well-monitored deployments.
        </p>
        <h3>A Community-Driven Path Forward</h3>
        <p>
          These ideas are not fixed commitments, but a reflection of where we believe privacy-respectful pDNS collection
          can - and should - go. Community feedback, real-world deployments, and collaboration will play a key role in shaping
          which of these directions become reality.
        </p>
      </div>
